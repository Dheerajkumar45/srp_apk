🛢️ Sucker Rod Pumping (SRP) System Architecture

The Sucker Rod Pumping (SRP) system—universally recognized by its surface walking-beam pumping unit ("pump jack")—is the flagship artificial lift technology in global petroleum production, operating on more than 80% of artificially lifted oil wells worldwide.

An SRP unit functions as a positive-displacement mechanical lift system. At the surface, a prime mover drives a double-reduction gearbox that rotates heavy crank counterweights. Twin pitman arms convert rotary crank motion into vertical reciprocation of the walking beam. The circular-sector horsehead and braided wireline bridle maintain exact axial alignment with the wellbore centerline as the chrome-plated polished rod reciprocates through the high-pressure stuffing box gland.

Downhole, a tapered high-tensile steel sucker rod string (API 66, 76, or 86) and concentrated bottom sinker bars (1.0"–1.5" OD) transmit surface motion to a subsurface plunger pump containing a Traveling Valve (TV) and a stationary Standing Valve (SV). In deviated wellbores, standard industry practice deploys factory-moulded polymer rod guides (moulded guided rods) across intervals where the Dogleg Severity (DLS) meets or exceeds $1^\circ$ per $30\text{ meters}$ to centralize the rod string, prevent metal-on-metal tubing wear, and reduce Coulomb drag.
